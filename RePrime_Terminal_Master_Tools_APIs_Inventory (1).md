# **Coverage Notes & Identified Gaps**

## **Categories Where Verification Was Thin**

Title and survey technology remains under-mapped. No dominant AI vendor exists for title commitment review, survey exception analysis, or ALTA reconciliation at the same maturity as lease abstraction or CMBS surveillance tools. This layer is still fragmented across lawyers, title companies, and general contract-AI platforms.

Property condition assessment (PCA) and engineering report automation have not been mapped at the same depth as lease, underwriting, and environmental tools. Consultant-heavy workflows — structural assessments, MEP reviews, roofing condition reports — lack a clear AI vendor category.

Municipal entitlement and permitting tooling beyond LightBox and DeepBlocks deserves expansion. Zoning and entitlement issues determine the upside case in value-add and development deals.

## **Pricing Transparency Gap**

Institutional CRE data remains universally opaque above the mid-market tier. Beyond Reonomy ($4,800/yr), AscendixRE ($79-99/mo), Apollo.io ($49-119/mo), and a handful of others, virtually every institutional-grade platform requires custom enterprise quotes. A dedicated pricing research pass should focus purely on API access costs, licensing limits, volume tiers, and whether each vendor can be integrated into a live Terminal workflow without custom enterprise friction.

## **Climate and Catastrophe Signal Expansion**

Hurricane, tornado, hail, and wildfire data have been captured across NOAA, First Street, HazardHub, and ClimateCheck. However, granular insurance cost modeling — actual premium data by property type, geography, and carrier — remains a gap. Insurance cost is a direct cap-rate driver and should be modeled as precisely as NOI. Emerging data sources for parametric insurance triggers and re-insurance pricing should be investigated.
